# 7. Conclusion

The central contribution of this monograph is to show that behavioral competence and boundary discrimination are not interchangeable. A system can be fluent and still be structurally under-grounded. Measuring only output-level task success is therefore insufficient; evaluation must also account for whether the model preserves the invariant boundaries that make its behavior lawful, stable, and recoverable.

The core principle is simple:

Observability defines recoverability.

Optimization can only repair what observation can see. Once a system is disrupted in unobserved directions, standard empirical-risk minimization will restore visible outputs while leaving latent invariant structure degraded unless additional structural feedback is explicitly supplied.

This is the mechanism behind Coherent Delusion and the reason that recoverability must be treated as an observability problem before it is an optimization problem.
